Shopify Tracking

A conversion-focused landing page that turns curious visitors into sign-ups.

Tracking SaaS product
Role

Timeline

Deliverables

View Prototype
01 — Overview & Problem

The problem

The client hired me to design a landing page that presents their tracking solution’s features and benefits compellingly — built to drive conversions and engage potential customers.

The challenge: capture attention and build trust fast, clearly communicating the product’s value while optimising for a high conversion rate.

01

Visitors needed to grasp what the product does — and why it matters — in seconds.

02

As a newer product, it had to overcome a trust barrier quickly.

03

Weak or scattered CTAs would mean interested visitors slipping away.

02 — Research

Understanding what converts

I studied high-performing SaaS landing pages and spoke with target customers about what makes them trust — and act on — a product they’ve just discovered.

Objectives

  • Goal 01Understand what the product does and who it’s for, from the brief.
  • Goal 02Learn what makes customers trust and sign up for a new tool.
  • Goal 03Benchmark strong SaaS landing pages for conversion patterns.

Methods

Competitor analysis User interviews Usability testing
10
SaaS landing pages benchmarked
6
target customers interviewed
5
participants in usability testing

Key insights

01

Lead with the outcome

People want the benefit up front — what the tool does for them — before any feature detail.

02

Trust before sign-up

Reviews, logos and clear numbers were the strongest nudges toward acting.

03

One job per section

Pages that built one clear idea at a time — ending in a CTA — converted best.

“If I don’t get what it does in the first few seconds, I’m gone.”

— Target customer, interview
03 — Users & Personas

Who the page converts

From interviews and the brief, three kinds of merchant kept surfacing — each weighing the product against a different priority before signing up.

The Store Owner
Solo merchant · busy
Goal
A tool that clearly saves time and pays for itself.
Frustration
Vague pages that bury the actual benefit.
The Ops Manager
Scaling brand · evaluating
Goal
Proof it’s reliable before bringing it to the team.
Frustration
No reviews or data to judge credibility.
The Side-Hustler
New seller · price-aware
Goal
Quick value and a clear, low-risk way to start.
Frustration
Unclear pricing or no obvious next step.

How might we…

make the product’s value obvious within seconds?
build trust with proof before asking to sign up?
guide every visitor toward one clear conversion?

09 — Usability Testing

Validating the page

What I tested

  • ✓ Do visitors grasp the product’s value within seconds?
  • ✓ Does the page build enough trust to sign up?
  • ✓ Is the call-to-action clear from anywhere on the page?

What I found

Visitors understood the value quickly and found the CTA with ease. A couple wanted clearer pricing and stronger proof higher up — both refined before final handoff.

“I knew exactly what it did and what to do next.”Tester 01
“The reviews made me actually believe it.”Tester 03
“Clean, clear, and the sign-up button was always right there.”Tester 05
10 — Outcome & Reflection

Results & what I learned

The page gives the product a sharp, trustworthy first impression — visitors understand the value fast and are guided, section by section, toward a single clear sign-up.

Takeaways

Lead with value

The benefit up front earns the attention everything else depends on.

Proof persuades

Reviews and numbers did more for conversion than extra copy ever could.

One job per section

Building one idea at a time kept the page focused and easy to act on.

This project sharpened how I write and structure for conversion — treating every section as a step in an argument that ends in “yes.”
