01

The problem

Guidex came to me with a simple landing page. They didn’t know yet what they wanted it to look like. No colours, no fonts, nothing.

The product is a compliance tool for UK fire engineers, so the website had to feel trustworthy. And I wasn’t only designing it. I was building it too, with Claude Code.

Besides the website, I also designed the chat page, project pages and other screens inside the Guidex app. They aren’t released yet, so I can’t show them here.

02

Where the design went

My first version was modern and techy, with a lot of movement: a 3D floor plan floating over the standards, animated steps, grainy gradients. I wanted the animation to show what the product does before anyone had to read about it.

Then the founders spoke to potential clients and realised most of them are older engineers. They’re used to calm, simple websites, and mine was a bit much for them. So I toned it down: a dark video hero at the top, clean white sections below, and one green accent colour. I still tried to keep some of my own style in it.

Honestly, the 3D version is still my favourite. But it wasn’t built for me.

03

How I worked with Claude

I designed every section in Figma before any code was written. Then I worked through the homepage with Claude one section at a time. For each one, I shared a screenshot of my design and explained in words how the layout should work. Once it matched my design, I moved on to the next section.

04

The hardest part: the review section

The review card sits inside a grid of crosshairs. Only one card has the orange background at a time, and the corners of its cell light up orange too. When you click next, the card fades out and the next review appears in the other diagonal cell.

Try 1

“Make a 2x2 flexbox”

I shared my design and asked for a 2x2 flexbox. Claude built a grid, but it couldn’t read the design properly from the image. The crosshairs didn’t sit where the lines cross and moved around at different screen sizes. It also kept missing the two tricky bits: only one card is coloured, and next moves it to the other diagonal cell.

Try 2

Words first, then small fixes

So I stopped leaning on the image and explained it in words, one rule at a time: where the lines go, which cell the card sits in, which corners light up and what next does. After that it was small fixes until it matched my Figma. On screens under 900px, I designed the card to sit centred over the grid instead of inside a cell.

How I’d brief it todaywritten for this page, not my original message
Build the review section on one shared grid, so the lines,
the crosshairs and the card always line up.

LAYOUT
- Thin grey lines, with a "+" crosshair wherever two lines cross.
- Two cells on a diagonal: top-left and bottom-right.
- The review card fills exactly one of those cells.

ONE CARD AT A TIME
- Only the card with the review gets the orange background.
- The two corner crosshairs of that cell turn orange. The rest stay grey.

NEXT / PREVIOUS
- The card fades out and the next review appears in the other cell.
- The counter updates (01 / 06).

SMALLER SCREENS (under 900px)
- The card sits centred over the grid instead of inside a cell.

DONE WHEN
- On desktop, every crosshair sits exactly where two lines cross,
  and stays there when the window is resized.
- Only one card is ever coloured, and next always moves it
  to the other cell.
- It matches my Figma in Chrome and Safari, and works on iPad and phone.

What I took from it: Claude reads words better than it reads a complicated picture. For a tricky layout, spelling out the rules and what “done” looks like worked much better than a screenshot on its own.

05

Three things I caught

Hero

A line I never designed

Claude added a thin, faint line to the hero on its own. You could only see it when you moved your mouse over it. I noticed because I was actually using the page, and had it removed.

Hero

A laggy video

The hero plays a full-screen video, and scrolling over it felt laggy in Chrome on my desktop. I flagged it to Claude, and the effects on the video were swapped for ones the browser only has to draw once. I checked it again before moving on.

Gradient

A gradient Claude couldn’t match

Claude Code kept getting my gradient colours wrong. So I switched tools: I gave my gradient image to Claude Design and asked it to write the code, then brought that code back into Claude Code. After that the colours matched my design exactly.

06

How I direct Claude and check its work

My Figma is what “done” looks like

Everything is designed before Claude writes any code. That gives me something exact to check against, so I can tell when a section is close but not quite right.

On Guidex: Claude kept missing that only one review card is coloured (04).

Rules when a picture isn’t enough

When Claude can’t read a layout from a screenshot, I change how I explain it and write out each rule in plain words.

On Guidex: the review section’s crosshair grid (04).

Use it like a visitor would

I scroll, hover and click through everything Claude builds, then check any fix again myself before moving on. Some problems only show up when you actually use the page.

On Guidex: the hover line Claude added on its own, and the laggy hero video (05).

Switch tools when one gets stuck

If Claude Code keeps missing one piece, I try a different tool for just that piece and bring the result back.

On Guidex: my gradient, which I got right through Claude Design (05).

Keep the code light and readable

I ask Claude to comment each section as it builds it, so if something breaks or needs changing later, I can find it fast. I push for the simplest way to build things, and at the end I read back through the code to make sure I still understand it and there’s no dead code left behind.

On Guidex: the live site’s code is commented section by section.
