Club App

A playful mobile home for a campus club — where students discover events and stay involved with a tap.

01 — Overview & Problem

The problem

A club app made with friends, where I was the UI/UX designer: a mobile app that engages students with a fun, intuitive interface and easy access to club events, updates and member resources.

The real challenge was balancing a playful, engaging look with genuinely user-friendly functionality — making staying involved feel effortless rather than like admin.

01

Club info was scattered across emails, group chats and notice boards.

02

Students struggled to discover events and keep track of what was on.

03

Engagement dropped whenever staying involved felt like effort.

02 — Research

Listening to students

I spoke with students about how they currently find out about club life, and studied the apps they already love — then pressure-tested the design with real users.

Objectives

  • Goal 01Understand how students currently hear about club events.
  • Goal 02Learn what would make them engage more often.
  • Goal 03Benchmark popular student & community apps for what works.

Methods

Key insights

01

Discovery is everything

Students miss events simply because they never see them in time — not because they don’t care.

02

Make it feel social

Seeing who else was going was the single biggest driver of “I’d turn up to that.”

03

Low effort, high reward

One-tap RSVP and reminders mattered far more than piling on extra features.

“Honestly, I’d join way more things if I just knew they were happening.”

— Student, interview
03 — Users & Personas

Who I designed for

From the interviews, three kinds of student kept coming up — each with a different relationship to the club, and a different reason to open the app.

The New Student
First year · finding their people
Goal
Discover clubs and events to settle in and make friends.
Frustration
Doesn’t know what’s on or where to look.
The Active Member
Regular · already involved
Goal
Quick event info, RSVPs and reminders in one place.
Frustration
Misses things buried in long group chats.
The Organiser
Committee · runs events
Goal
Reach members and get a clear headcount.
Frustration
Low turnout when posts don’t reach people.

How might we…

help students discover events they’d actually care about?
make joining in feel social and effortless?
keep members engaged between events?

08 — Usability Testing

Validating the design

What I tested

  • ✓ Can a student find an event and RSVP in seconds?
  • ✓ Is moving between tabs intuitive?
  • ✓ Does the app feel fun and worth opening again?

What I found

Students RSVP’d quickly and enjoyed the playful feel. A couple wanted clearer event categories and a visible “who’s going” count — both refined before the final screens.

“Found an event and RSVP’d in like five seconds.”Tester 01
“It actually feels fun, not like admin.”Tester 03
09 — Outcome & Reflection

Results & what I learned

The concept turns scattered club comms into one playful hub — students can discover events, see who’s going, and stay involved with a single tap.

Takeaways

Discovery first

Surfacing the right thing at the right time beats adding more features.

Make it social

Social proof — who’s going — was the strongest pull to engage.

Keep it effortless

Every tap I removed from the core action made people more likely to take it.

Designing for students pushed me to make something genuinely fun without losing clarity — and to cut anything that didn’t serve discovery or engagement.
